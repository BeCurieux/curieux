# The Two-Million Shortlist

*Sounding Labs · strategy note · as at 29 August 2026*

Ten bets that can plausibly reach $2m a year with one person on the tools —
ranked, costed, and with the ones that can't get there said out loud.

Every opportunity here is scored against the same question: **what does one
person have to sell, to how many people, at what price, to clear $2m?** Ideas
that only work with a sales team, a marketing budget or a co-founder are not
opportunities for you — they are opportunities for somebody else.

Four of the ten already exist in some form in this repo. Six do not. The
ranking does not care which.

---

## The arithmetic, first

$2,000,000 ARR = $167k MRR.

| Price | Accounts needed | Verdict |
|---|---:|---|
| $49/mo | 3,400 | Needs a distribution machine you don't have |
| $99/mo | 1,685 | Top 1% of the Shopify App Store |
| $199/mo | 838 | Support alone is a full-time job |
| **$500/mo** | **334** | Reachable solo, with a real channel |
| **$1,000/mo** | **167** | The sweet spot. You can know them all |
| **$2,500/mo** | **67** | Needs a buyer with a compliance budget |

**The solo $2m band sits at $500–$2,000/month and 150–350 accounts.** Below
$200/month the number of humans you need to acquire, onboard and support
exceeds what one person can carry, however good the product is. The Shopify
data says the same thing from the other end: the top 1% of listed apps clear
$1m ARR, so $2m is the top fraction of a percent — and those apps are run by
teams of two to fifteen.

Which points somewhere specific: **regulated buyers with a budget line, not
merchants with an app allowance.** Six of the ten below are compliance
products. That is not a theme I went looking for; it is what survives the
arithmetic.

## The ten

Ranked by probability × speed × what is already built.

### 01 · Franca's engine, sold as a corpus platform
**29 days to ECGT**

The asset is not the claim scanner. It is a deterministic, citable rule engine
with a beautiful artefact on the end — and every regulated body of words in
this country is another corpus you can run through it.

- **The maths** — Franca alone at $49–199 caps out around $1m. The platform
  version, six corpora × 60 accounts × $700/mo, is $2.5m from one codebase.
- **Why you** — 292 tests, no network, no API key. The engine is done. The
  scarce input is regulatory knowledge for authoring corpora, which is the
  thing you actually have.
- **Aussie edge** — Australia is small enough that one person can hold ACCC,
  TGA, FSANZ, Fair Work and the APPs at once. In the US you'd need a firm to
  author against half that.
- **First move** — ship the ECGT corpus and run the countdown this fortnight.
  Then author corpus #2 and *time yourself*.
- **Kill signal** — corpus #2 costs more than a quarter of what corpus #1 cost.
  If each corpus is a rebuild you have one product, not a platform, and the
  ceiling is $1m.

### 02 · AML/CTF Tranche 2, program-in-a-box
**Live · day 59**

Roughly 80,000 real estate agents, conveyancers, accountants, lawyers and
precious-metal dealers became reporting entities on 1 July. Most bought a Word
template off their peak body and filed it. Penalties run to $33m for a body
corporate.

- **The maths** — 400 firms at $450/mo is $2.16m. Or 150 mid-size firms at
  $1,200. Off a base of 80,000 that is a 0.5% share.
- **Why you** — identical product shape to Franca: a rules corpus, a generated
  program document, an evidence trail, and an annual review that renews itself.
- **Aussie edge** — total. Every global AML vendor is priced for banks. Nobody
  is priced for a two-partner conveyancer in Ballarat.
- **First move** — twenty calls into conveyancers only: the most homogeneous
  captured group, the most panicked, and reachable through state institutes.
- **Kill signal** — firms say the template is enough and they've already lodged
  it. Then the pain is filing, not maintaining, and filing is a one-off.

### 03 · Waterline as AU-native margin truth

Every margin tool models US shipping and US payment fees. None models Australia
Post zones, the 4–6% Afterpay and Zip haircut, or ad spend billed in USD
against revenue earned in AUD. For an Australian brand that is not a feature
gap — it is most of the margin.

- **The maths** — 300 stores at $550/mo is $1.98m. Better shape: 40 agencies at
  $4,200/mo running portfolios — same revenue, a fortieth of the support.
- **Why you** — real ingested Shopify data with real cost-per-item in AUD is
  already in the repo. Engine, guardrails and reversible ledger exist.
- **Aussie edge** — AU DTC brands are small enough that a 3% margin error
  decides the year, and big enough to pay $500/mo to find it.
- **First move** — connect Meta ad spend. The README says spend is 0 and ship
  cost is 0; margin without ad spend is not margin, and no merchant will
  believe the number until it is in.
- **Kill signal** — merchants agree the number is true and change nothing.
  Insight nobody acts on does not renew.

### 04 · Payday Super assurance, sold to the bookkeeper
**Live · day 59**

Since 1 July, super has to reach the fund within seven business days of payday,
every payday. The employer does not watch that. The bookkeeper does, across
forty client files, and wears the blame when one slips.

- **The maths** — 600 bookkeepers at $300/mo covering their whole book is
  $2.16m. You sell to one person and cover forty employers.
- **Why you** — small, mechanical, reconciliation-shaped, deeply unglamorous.
  Exactly what one person ships properly and a platform team ships badly.
- **Aussie edge** — purely Australian. STP now carries YTD qualifying earnings
  and super liability at every payday, so the data source is a government feed.
- **First move** — build the cross-client exception view, not a super
  calculator. "Which of my 47 clients missed the seven days this week" is the
  only screen that matters.
- **Kill signal** — Xero ships a multi-client super exception dashboard. They
  sell per-file so they probably won't, but this is the one bet with a named
  incumbent standing on top of it.

### 05 · AASB S2, and the questionnaire landing underneath it
**306 days to Group 3**

Group 3 entities report from 1 July 2027. But the money is earlier and further
down: every SME supplying a Group 1 or 2 company is already being sent
emissions questionnaires it has no idea how to answer. Answer once, reuse for
every customer who asks.

- **The maths** — 250 suppliers at $700/mo is $2.1m. Group 3 alone is thousands
  of entities at ≥$50m revenue or ≥100 staff.
- **Why you** — same engine shape: a standard, a corpus of required
  disclosures, a generated artefact, an assurance trail an auditor accepts.
- **Aussie edge** — AASB S2 is an Australian standard with an Australian
  phase-in and Australian assurance requirements. A global carbon-accounting
  tool answers a different question.
- **First move** — get three real questionnaires Group 1 companies have
  actually sent suppliers. The product is the answer sheet, not the carbon maths.
- **Kill signal** — SMEs forward the questionnaire to their accountant and
  consider it handled. Then the buyer is the accounting firm, and it is a
  slower product.

### 06 · Support at Home, for small and regional providers
**Live · day 301**

The Aged Care Act 2024 and Support at Home replaced Home Care Packages on
1 November 2025 — single provider model, new service classifications, a new
claiming interface with Services Australia. Big providers bought AlayaCare.
Small and regional ones got a grant and a PDF.

- **The maths** — 150 providers at $1,200/mo is $2.16m. Highest ACV here, and
  the fewest customers you would need to know by name.
- **Why you** — rules, deadlines, generated evidence, a portal integration.
  Fourth appearance of the same shape.
- **Aussie edge** — absolute. PRODA, the Aged Care Provider Portal, My Aged
  Care — none of this machinery exists anywhere else.
- **First move** — only after #2 or #4 is earning. Slowest sale on the list:
  boards, procurement, clinical governance. Budget nine months to first dollar.
- **Kill signal** — three providers in a row say they can't buy from a sole
  trader. In aged care your bus factor is a legitimate procurement objection.

### 07 · POPUUP — build it to be bought

Highest ceiling, lowest solo probability. Linktree, LoudCrowd and Superfiliate
are funded and in market. The open position you identified — the simple extreme
— is real, but claiming it is a distribution race, and distribution races are
the specific thing a solo founder loses.

- **The maths** — as ARR, 1,700 merchants at $99, top-1% territory. As an
  outcome, a $2m+ acquisition is more probable and needs 1,700 of nothing.
- **Why you** — the Catalogue Genome and the decision-provenance log are the
  acquirable assets. The page renderer is not; anyone can build a renderer.
- **Aussie edge** — Linktree is a Melbourne company with a Shopify commerce
  product and a conspicuous hole where AI merchandising should sit. Your most
  likely acquirer is a domestic flight away.
- **First move** — instrument the Genome, not the funnel. "Our merchandising
  beat the merchant's own ordering by X%" is the sentence that gets a meeting.
- **Kill signal** — ninety days in, merchants who know their catalogue call the
  merchandising *plausible* rather than *right*. Plausible is what every
  competitor already ships.

### 08 · The Privacy Act corpus
**103 days**

From 10 December every Australian business must disclose its use of automated
decision-making in its privacy policy. There are hundreds of thousands of
privacy policies in this country and almost none say anything true about AI.
Scan, finding, rewrite, badge — exactly the product already built.

- **The maths** — 300 accounts at $300/mo is $1.08m, under the bar alone. It
  clears $2m as part of #1. Listed separately for what it proves, not what it
  earns.
- **Why you** — cheapest possible test of the platform thesis. Different
  regulator, different corpus, same engine, same card, same funnel.
- **Aussie edge** — the ADM obligation is Australian and dated. Tranche 2 of the
  reforms, removing the $3m small business exemption, is still coming and would
  multiply this market by an order of magnitude.
- **First move** — author it as corpus #2 in #1. Three weeks, timed.
- **Kill signal** — it takes longer than three weeks. Same signal as #1, which
  is the point of running it.

### 09 · Security of Payment claims for subbies

Every state has its own Security of Payment Act, with its own reference dates,
response windows and adjudication clocks. Subcontractors lose real money to a
payment claim served a day late or missing a required statement. Nobody has
built this because it is unglamorous and fragmented eight ways — which is the
entire moat.

- **The maths** — 400 head contractors at $450/mo is $2.16m and is the workable
  shape. 3,000 individual subbies at $60 is the same revenue and ten times the
  support.
- **Why you** — a deadline engine with a generated document on the end. Fifth
  appearance of the shape; at this point it is a competence, not a coincidence.
- **Aussie edge** — eight different Acts inside one country. Functionally
  un-enterable by anyone who does not live here.
- **First move** — NSW and QLD only. Two Acts, most of the market, and you find
  out whether the deadline engine is the product before authoring six more.
- **Kill signal** — subbies won't pay because they are certain the problem is
  the builder, not their paperwork. They may be right, and being right does not
  make them buyers.

### 10 · Lattice — the one Shopify category where $2m is normal

Bundles and subscriptions is the category where apps demonstrably clear seven
figures. It is also the most crowded shelf in the store. The reason it stays on
the list is weakest-link inventory gating: the thing every bundle app gets
wrong and every merchant with multi-warehouse stock feels weekly.

- **The maths** — 1,700 merchants at $99 is top-1% territory. 350 at $400,
  merchants with genuine inventory complexity, is $1.7m and a better fight.
- **Why you** — the gating engine and reversible action log are built, and
  gating is a real differentiator rather than a positioning one.
- **Aussie edge** — none worth claiming. This competes on merit against the
  whole world, which is why it is tenth and not fourth.
- **First move** — nothing, for now. Right product, wrong queue position. It
  needs App Store SEO and 200+ reviews: eighteen months of compounding you
  cannot start six times over.
- **Kill signal** — you can't get one merchant to name weakest-link gating as
  the reason they switched. Without that it is a me-too bundle app, and there
  are ninety of those.

## The bench

Good work; not a $2m path.

**Haunted Wishlist** — genuinely charming, and $49/mo × 3,400 merchants is not
a solo path. Its $2m version is not a wishlist app; it is web-push retention
infrastructure priced on sends. As-is it is the most acquirable thing you own,
and that is a fine reason to keep it alive without feeding it.

**Qotidia** — the hardest $2m per unit of effort here: print margins, consumer
CAC, a two-month seasonal window. Only defensible if the Little Printabelles
list converts to a $100+ product cold. That is one email to find out, worth
sending before another build sprint goes in.

**ClaimKind** — let its November kill criterion adjudicate it, as written. The
failure mode is rescuing it with attention Franca needs; a sales-led $1k/mo
product and a PLG launch cannot share one person's quarter.

---

## The call: you can't do ten, do two

**Now → 27 September.** Franca's ECGT corpus and the countdown. It is the only
dated demand event in front of you, it is 29 days out, and the engine is built.
Miss it and you wait a year for another one this good.

**October → March.** One second corpus — AML/CTF (#2) or Payday Super (#4). Not
two. Author it, time it, and let the effort decide whether #1 is a product or a
platform. That single measurement is worth more than any of the market sizing
above.

Everything else is a bench, and a bench is a perfectly good place for good work
to sit. What kills a solo founder is not picking the wrong bet — it is running
seven products at fourteen percent attention each and calling it a portfolio.
There are seven directories in this repo.
