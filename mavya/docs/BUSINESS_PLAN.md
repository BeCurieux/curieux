# Business Plan — The Operating System for Recurring Family Activities

> Saved from the founder's business plan (30 September 2026). The plan is the
> founder's; `docs/STRATEGY.md` turns it into rules for building, and the
> notes at the end say where today's build stands against it. Figures here

Sep 30, 2026 · @Lo

**Working concept:** a modern operating platform for swim schools first, then other recurring children's activities.

**To providers:** Less admin. Fuller classes. Happier families.

**To families:** Everything your kids do, in one place.

## 1. Executive summary

The business starts by solving one specific, expensive problem for swim schools: **too much human administration is required to manage recurring classes.**

Current systems record scheduling, enrolments, payments, attendance, makeups, waitlists and parent communication. The opportunity is to go further. Rather than giving staff software to *perform* administrative work, the platform will increasingly perform that work itself.

A parent reporting an absence should not create another task for reception. Instead:

- Vacancy created
- Eligible replacement identified
- Offer sent
- Vacancy filled
- Makeup entitlement handled
- Class list updated
- Instructor notified
- Family informed

**Staff intervention: ideally zero.** This automation-first philosophy is the foundation of the company.

### The problem

Every week a class-based business generates hundreds of small administrative events: absences, cancellations, makeup requests, waitlists, class moves, progression, instructor changes, payment failures, refunds, credits, sibling scheduling, parent questions, enrolments, trials, term changes, closures and communications.

Most platforms record these events. Many still need a human to decide what happens next. The result is a business with sophisticated software that still runs on reception staff, email, phone calls and manual decisions.

### The opportunity

Not prettier class-management software — a different operating model. Every workflow should answer one question: **why does a human need to perform this task at all?** Wherever the rules can be understood, the system acts, and only exceptions requiring judgement reach a person.

| Software as database | Software as operator |
|---|---|
| Records the absence | Fills the vacancy |
| Lists the waitlist | Makes the offer |
| Flags the failed payment | Recovers it |
| Shows the empty spot | Sells it |

Swimming is the beachhead because it combines recurring weekly classes, limited capacity, progression levels, waitlists, makeups, siblings, recurring payments, instructors and high administrative volume. Once proven, the same engine supports gymnastics, dance, martial arts, tennis, music, tutoring and other recurring programs.

The long-term ambition: **the infrastructure layer for recurring family activities.**

## 2. Beachhead market: swimming first

The initial customer is an independent or small multi-site Australian swim school — large enough for inefficiency to be expensive, small enough to buy quickly.

### Ideal customer profile

| Attribute | Target |
|---|---|
| Swimmers | 300–2,500 |
| Locations | 1–5 |
| Enrolment model | Recurring weekly, recurring billing |
| Staffing | Several instructors, a receptionist or admin function |
| Operations | Progression levels, waiting lists, meaningful absence/makeup volume |
| Decision-maker | Owner or manager actively involved in operations |

### Why swimming

Swimming contains most of the operational complexity the platform needs to prove. Automate it well and the core transfers to other activities.

| Characteristic | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Capacity constraints | A class cannot hold unlimited swimmers |
| Progression | Children move through levels |
| Recurring enrolment | Families attend week after week |
| Makeups | Absences create future entitlement |
| Waitlists | Popular classes generate excess demand |
| Staff ratios | Instructor-to-student ratios are enforced |
| Payments | Recurring fees must be collected reliably |
| Family scheduling | Parents often have several children |

## 3. Product

Two connected experiences: an operating system for the provider and a portal for the family.

### Provider operating system

The business manages customers, children, classes, locations, instructors, enrolments, payments, attendance, progression, makeups, credits, waitlists, communication, reporting, retention, trials and capacity — with routine administration increasingly handled automatically.

### Family portal

An exceptionally simple consumer experience. Parents see every child and every activity in one place and manage schedules, payments, absences, makeups, waitlists, class changes, progression, messages, permissions, carers, medical details, receipts and bookings. Eventually one account works across multiple providers.

### The hero workflow: makeups without the admin

The first product should become famous for one thing. Sophie cannot attend Thursday's Level 4 lesson; her parent taps **Can't attend**. The platform then:

- Records the absence
- Checks the school's makeup rules
- Issues the appropriate entitlement
- Opens the vacant class position
- Finds compatible swimmers
- Ranks eligible families
- Sends an offer
- Temporarily reserves the place
- Books the replacement
- Updates attendance
- Updates the instructor's class list
- Notifies both families

**Reception does nothing.** This is the defining demonstration of the product.

### Product philosophy

Every major feature must do at least one of five things; features that do none are not prioritised.

- Reduce administration
- Increase capacity utilisation
- Increase revenue
- Improve the family experience
- Strengthen the platform's data or network advantage

### Roadmap

| Phase | Focus | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Swim MVP | One vertical, one killer workflow | Provider and parent accounts, children, instructors, locations, classes, recurring schedules, capacities, levels, enrolments, attendance, absence reporting, makeup credits, waitlists, class changes, payments, family ledger, messaging, progression, instructor mobile view, basic reporting — and the **automated absence → vacancy → refill workflow** |
| 2 — Operating intelligence | The system starts deciding | Automated progression recommendations, payment recovery, retention signals, smarter waitlists, instructor substitution, capacity optimisation, advanced reporting, multi-site management, migration tooling, workflow automation |
| 3 — Other activities | Same engine, vertical interfaces | Gymnastics, cheer, dance, martial arts, tennis, other academies |
| 4 — Universal family account | One identity across providers | One account, one calendar, one family profile, multiple providers |
| 5 — Discovery marketplace | Provider density enables demand | "Jack is free Saturday morning" → beginner gymnastics, 9:30am, 1.8km away, trial available |

## 6. Defensibility

The initial software is not highly defensible. The moat must be deliberately accumulated through six strategic assets.

| Asset | How it compounds |
|---|---|
| Activity automation engine | Every workflow adds rules, exceptions and decision logic; the platform becomes extremely good at operating recurring class businesses |
| Family graph | A secure model linking family → carers → children → activities → providers → payments → schedules → permissions → progression; more valuable as families use multiple providers |
| Migration engine | Importing and normalising data from competing systems; switching to us becomes easy while competitors still face the migration problem we have solved repeatedly |
| Payments | Deep payment integration embeds the system in daily operations |
| Proprietary operational data | Cancellation behaviour, vacancy conversion, demand patterns, utilisation, progression, payment behaviour, retention, capacity — improves automation and benchmarking |
| Network effects | More providers → more families → greater family utility → more providers → more data → better automation. The strongest long-term moat |

## 7. Go-to-market

Not a traditional SaaS launch. The sequence is **Learn → Pilot → Prove → Publish results → Sell repeatedly.** The first goal is not thousands of users; it is **five swim schools that never want to go back.**

### Founder-led sales

For roughly the first 50 customers, sales remain founder-led. The founders must personally learn why customers buy, hesitate, switch and stay, what they value, and which workflows hurt most. That knowledge becomes the sales playbook.

### Founding School Program

Recruit about five founding swim schools.

| We offer | We ask for |
|---|---|
| White-glove setup | Genuine product use |
| Direct founder access | Detailed feedback |
| Assisted migration | Workflow access |
| Early automation capability | Permission to measure results |
| Influence over product development | A case study if successful |
| Preferential founding pricing |  |

### Prospecting

Build a carefully qualified list of Australian swim schools — independent operators and sophisticated multi-site independents with 300–2,500 swimmers, visible admin complexity, established parent portals, recurring payments and waitlist/makeup policies. No mass emailing; the process is highly targeted.

### Outreach

The first approach asks for a conversation, not a sale:

We're building a platform designed to automate the administration around absences, makeups, waitlists and class moves. We're speaking with a small number of swim-school operators and watching how these workflows actually happen.

### Discovery

Ask operators to demonstrate real workflows rather than list feature requests:

- Show me what happened when someone cancelled yesterday.
- Show me how a makeup is booked.
- Show me what happens to the empty spot.
- Show me how you move someone to the next level.
- Show me a failed payment.
- Show me how you schedule two siblings.

### Quantify the problem

Every workflow converts into money, which becomes the basis of ROI-based selling.

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Makeup requests per week | 45 |
| Minutes per request | 7 |
| Staff hours per week | 5.25 |
| Loaded wage | A$35/hour |
| Annual cost | ≈ A$9,500 |
| Value if 80% automated | ≈ A$7,600/year |

### The demo

Not a product tour. Start with the customer's biggest operational problem: a parent taps *Can't attend Thursday*, and the platform records the absence, creates the vacancy, identifies an eligible swimmer, accepts the offer, refills the class, handles the makeup credit and updates everyone. **Staff actions required: 0.** Only then show the broader product.

### Overcoming the biggest barrier: migration

The largest objection will be *changing systems sounds painful*. So migration becomes part of the product: **you don't migrate, we migrate you.**

- Audit current system
- Receive or export data
- Map fields
- Import
- Validate
- Reconcile
- Staff review
- Test
- Cutover
- Support

### Pilot structure

No indefinite pilots. A 60-day pilot with defined success criteria measures absences, makeup requests, vacancies and refills, staff interventions, support contacts, payment recovery, administration time and parent usage — then compares before and after.

### Case studies

All numbers must be genuinely measured. A powerful case study looks like this:

| Measure | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Admin hours per week | 7.4 | 1.9 |
| Cancellations | 42 | 42 |
| Makeup requests | 38 | 38 |
| Routine workflows automated | — | 81% |
| Suitable vacancies refilled | 31% | 72% |

Outcome: 5.5 hours saved per week, higher utilisation, fewer parent calls. Far more compelling than a feature list.

### Signature lead magnet: the Swim School Admin Calculator

A provider enters swimmers, cancellations, makeup volume, admin wages, lesson value, occupancy and payment failure rate. The tool estimates **annual administrative cost** and **potential lost capacity revenue**, then asks: *See how much of this could be automated.*

### Signature industry report: the Australian Swim School Operations Report

Admin hours per 100 swimmers, cancellation rate, class utilisation, makeup usage, payment failures, waiting-list conversion, progression patterns, trial conversion. Initially from surveys and interviews; later from anonymised platform data. A PR, SEO, conference, sales and authority asset in one.

## 11. Team

The company stays lean for as long as possible. Permanent employees are added only when a clear bottleneck appears.

| Role | Responsible for |
|---|---|
| Founder / CEO | Product vision, strategy, sales, partnerships, major customer relationships |
| COO / second founder or partner | Implementation, customer success, migrations, operations, support, sales operations |
| AI | Coding, testing, documentation, support, analysis, product development, marketing production, internal operations |
| Contractors | Legal, privacy, security, accounting, technical audits, specialised engineering |

### Hiring philosophy

Do not hire because companies of a certain size are "supposed" to have particular employees. Hire when one of four bottlenecks appears:

- **Customer success** — onboarding or support prevents growth
- **Engineering** — product development prevents sales or retention
- **Sales** — qualified leads exceed founder capacity
- **Operations** — financial or compliance processes create risk

The first meaningful target is **A$1m ARR**. It proves customers will pay, the product retains them, acquisition is repeatable and the business has operating leverage. From there, **A$3m ARR** is the next major milestone, at which point the founders have considerable strategic flexibility.

### Acquisition value

The objective is not to build the company to sell it, but to build something strategically important enough that acquisition becomes an option. Assets a strategic buyer cannot easily reproduce: recurring SaaS revenue, low churn, payments volume, the activity automation engine, the family identity network, migration technology, proprietary operational data, multi-vertical architecture, provider distribution and the customer acquisition network.

## 13. Flywheel and north star

### The long-term flywheel

Providers bring families, families generate operational data, data improves automation, automation improves business outcomes, outcomes drive referrals, and referrals bring the next provider.

Once provider coverage is broad enough, a second loop starts: more coverage → a more useful family account → greater family adoption → provider demand to join → marketplace liquidity → more bookings → greater platform value. This is how the company evolves from vertical SaaS into a network.

### The first major proof point

The company needs one undeniable result — something like *82% of routine makeup administration now happens automatically*, *the school saved 26 administration hours last month*, or *vacancy recovery increased from 38% to 73%*. That result becomes the sales pitch, homepage, case study, conference talk, LinkedIn content, outbound message and partnership story. The entire early growth engine forms around it.

### The north star

| Stage | Message |
|---|---|
| Externally, initially | The smarter way to run a swim school |
| Behind the scenes | Build the automation engine for recurring activities |
| Later | Run your activity business almost automatically |
| Eventually | Everything your kids do, in one place |

The strategic journey: **swim-school software → activity operating system → family activity network → activity infrastructure platform.** Start extremely narrow while designing the architecture for the larger opportunity.

### Key risks

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Existing platforms copy features | Build accumulated workflow intelligence, network effects, payments and migration capability rather than relying on individual features |
| Operators resist switching | Make migration a core capability and quantify ROI |
| Product becomes too generic | Maintain deep vertical-specific experiences on shared architecture |
| Too much is built before validation | Start with one vertical and one killer automation workflow |
| Support becomes labour-intensive | Design onboarding, migration and support automation into the product early |
| Expansion occurs too quickly | Enter a second vertical only after strong retention and repeatable sales in swimming |

### Final principle

**Do not simply digitise administrative work. Eliminate it.**

Every provider added should strengthen at least one of: automation, migration capability, data intelligence, payments, the family network, the provider network. If that happens consistently, the company becomes more valuable and harder to compete with as it grows.

## Notes against the build (30 September 2026)

Where Ovyko stands against this plan, and what to decide:

1. **The hero workflow mostly exists.** Can't attend → credit → spot offered
   automatically to the best-fitting family → claimed in one tap → roster
   updated (M4–M5.5). Missing for "reception does nothing": telling families
   outside the app (email, M6c) and telling the instructor.
2. **Migration is started.** Classes, families, children and places import
   from spreadsheets, checked first, with counts to reconcile and an undo
   (M6a). Next: attendance, levels, credits and balances from real exports.
3. **Measure the headline number from day one.** "Automated Operations Rate"
   needs Ovyko to count each routine event and whether a person touched it,
   and the pilot's "before" baseline. Every action is already audited; the
   report isn't built yet. Build it before the first pilot starts.
4. **Keep payments out of the first pilots.** Moving families' direct-debit
   authorities is the hardest part of switching. Pilots keep their billing
   (as `docs/STRATEGY.md` says); payments and the family ledger follow (M7).
5. **ROI needs more than admin time.** A$7,600 a year saved against
   A$4,200–5,400 a year in fees is thin. Add families who didn't miss out,
   credits used instead of lapsing, and (once sold) casual-place revenue.
6. **Decide pricing.** This plan says A$349–449/month per location (Core);
   `docs/STRATEGY.md` had A$129/249/449. One story before the first sales
   call.
7. **Ratios.** The plan counts instructor ratios as core; Ovyko enforces
   class capacity only. Decide whether ratios are pilot-critical.
8. **Trust is a sales asset.** Children's data, Australian hosting, a school
   data agreement and a privacy summary for parents (M6) answer the first
   question a careful owner asks. Add it to the risks.
9. **AI stays out of v0.1** unless approved (`CLAUDE.md` rule 11); the "AI
   operational assistant" add-on belongs to a later phase.

## Notes on payments and government vouchers (3 October 2026)

The money in vertical software comes from four things: holding the
records, money flowing through it, rules customers fear getting wrong, and
spreading through a network. Ovyko already has the records and the rules
(child safety, privacy, audit). Payments add the money.

### Government kids' vouchers: the hook

Several states give families vouchers for sport and creative activities,
which providers must redeem. As of October 2026:

- **NSW Active and Creative Kids:** two A$50 vouchers per child a year, for
  sport, swimming, dance, music, art and more. Families get a voucher number
  from Service NSW. Registered providers redeem each one by hand in the
  Service NSW Business Bureau app, and the government pays the provider.
- **Queensland FairPlay:** redeemed by registered providers through the
  Queensland Government's online portal.
- **South Australia:** a sports voucher scheme exists; its provider process
  is not yet checked.

Today schools collect voucher numbers by email or at the desk, type each
one in, take the amount off the right family's bill and chase the ones that
fail, in bursts of hundreds when new vouchers open.

### How it works in Ovyko

1. Parents enter a voucher number when they re-enrol for the next term
   (M6e); Ovyko takes it off that child's fee at once.
2. The owner sees one list to redeem ("18 vouchers"), with each number next
   to the child.
3. The owner redeems them in the government's system and ticks them off.
   A failed voucher goes back on the family's bill and the parent is told.
4. Discounts, money owed by the government and money received reconcile.

Neither NSW nor Queensland appears to offer software a way to redeem
automatically, so the school still enters them in the government's system.
Ovyko removes the collecting, chasing, bill adjustments and reconciliation.
If a state opens a connection for software, Ovyko could do the last step
too; worth asking.

### Where the money is

The vouchers themselves are small (A$100 per child a year in NSW). They
are the reason schools want Ovyko to handle their fees. With payments (M7),
families pay the term fee in Ovyko, upfront or by instalment, less any
voucher; the money reaches the school through Stripe Connect, and Ovyko
takes a small share on top of the subscription. A school with 500 children
at about A$250 a term moves roughly A$500,000 a year.

The pitch: "Re-enrolment, fees and vouchers in one place. No chasing, no
spreadsheets." No competitor reviewed leads with voucher handling.

### To confirm before building

- Each state's provider rules: whether a voucher can go against a term fee,
  and how long the government takes to pay.
- Whether any state offers a connection for software to redeem directly.
- Stripe Connect set-up for Ovyko and its schools (M7).

Sources: service.nsw.gov.au (Active and Creative Kids, provider FAQs),
qld.gov.au (FairPlay activity providers).
