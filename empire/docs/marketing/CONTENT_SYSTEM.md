## 1. Voice in one paragraph

Write the way you'd text a friend: plain, specific, warm, no hype. Say the result, not the feature. Dry wit is welcome. Sarcasm aimed at a person is not. Every claim needs evidence or a label. Where we don't know something, we say so.

Reference lines (real voice):

- A photo brief isn't a photo.
- Feeling busy is not evidence. These three numbers are.
- Anyone selling a trick to "beat the algorithm" is guessing.
- You don't need a big audience to test an idea. You need five real conversations and one person willing to pay.
- Most side-income ideas don't fail because they're bad. They fail because they don't fit the week you actually have.

Australian spelling throughout (organise, colour, licence as a noun, practise as a verb).

## 4. The five franchises

### 4.1 The Audacity Department

**Purpose:** Name the patterns hype sellers use, so our audience spots them before handing over money or evenings. This is where we're most rebellious and most fun.

**Audience job-to-be-done:** "Help me tell a real opportunity from a sales pitch dressed as one, without feeling silly for being tempted."

**Formats:** Reels (text-on-screen with voice-over, "translation" style), carousels (red-flag lists, phrase translators).

**Cadence in the calendar:** 6 Reels + 3 carousels over 30 days (about 2 per week).

**Repeatable structure (beats):**

1. Hook: the generic pattern, quoted as a type, not a person ("The 'opportunity' DM").
2. Translation: what it actually means, one line.
3. Why it works on good people (time pressure, hope, tiredness). No mocking the audience.
4. The question that breaks it ("What do I have to buy before I can start?").
5. Soft CTA to the Fit Check red flags.

**Tone notes:** Dry, amused, never cruel. Punch at tactics, not people. The joke is on the pitch.

**Done well:**

- Generic, composite patterns written by us ("the screenshot of a big number with no costs shown").
- One clear question the viewer can use tomorrow.
- Pay-to-join schemes described plainly: "pay-to-join schemes", "schemes where you earn by signing people up", "the 'opportunity' DM", "the starter kit you have to buy".

**Avoid:**

- Naming, depicting, stitching, duetting or screenshotting any real person, brand or post.
- Recreating real people's captions word for word.
- Repeating the income claims we're criticising. Describe them ("a figure with a lot of zeros"), don't print them.
- Implying that everyone who joined something is foolish.

**Points to:** Free Fit Check (six red flags). Occasionally the Income Fit Test, max once a week overall.

### 4.2 Show Me the Numbers

**Purpose:** Make business maths visible and unscary: fees, refunds, GST thresholds, contribution per sale, break-even ad spend.

**Audience job-to-be-done:** "Show me what this would really cost and keep, before I commit."

**Formats:** Reels (screen recording of the calculator with hands/voice-over, or numbers typed on a notepad), carousels (step-by-step worked examples).

**Cadence in the calendar:** 5 Reels + 5 carousels over 30 days.

**Repeatable structure (beats):**

1. Hook: a sticker price vs what's kept, or a question ("Is a ROAS of 1.1 good?").
2. Label: "HYPOTHETICAL example" on screen before any number appears.
3. The maths, one line per step, in order.
4. The twist: where it's a loss, or where it's "not enough evidence yet".
5. Takeaway rule in one sentence.
6. Soft CTA to the calculator or Fit Check.

**Tone notes:** Calm, precise, slightly smug about subtraction. Numbers in Lora on graphics.

**Done well:**

- Every number labelled HYPOTHETICAL or FACT (checked date).
- Losses shown as plainly as wins.
- Currency always written (A$ or US$).
- The calculator shown working, not described.

**Avoid:**

- Any figure framed as what someone will make, or "typical results".
- Real sales or ad data from our account (we publish none).
- Tax advice. GST content says the threshold and "check the official rules for your situation".

**Points to:** Ads Profitability Calculator (studio.herlittleempire.com/ads/tools/calculator), Fit Check, Income Fit Test (per-idea calculator), max once a week.

### 4.3 Big Plans. Real Life.

**Purpose:** Fit a business into the week people actually have: school hours, day jobs, tired evenings, unpaid hours, saying no.

**Audience job-to-be-done:** "Help me work out what I can realistically do with my time, without guilt or a 5am routine."

**Formats:** Reels (desk, kettle, notebook, calendar b-roll with voice-over), carousels (week audits, Fit Check questions unpacked).

**Cadence in the calendar:** 7 Reels + 2 carousels over 30 days.

**Repeatable structure (beats):**

1. Hook: a relatable truth about time ("Evenings are not free time.").
2. Make it concrete: count it, block it, or list it.
3. The reframe: what fits, what doesn't, what to stop.
4. One small action for this week.
5. Soft CTA (Fit Check question 1, 2, 3 or 5) and sign-off.

**Tone notes:** Warm, practical, a bit wry. Permission, not pep talk.

**Done well:**

- Specific blocks ("a reliable 40 minutes on Tuesday and Thursday").
- Unpaid hours named (messages, admin, listings, posting, packing, learning the tool).
- Saying no treated as a business skill.

**Avoid:**

- Hustle framing, early-alarm routines, "grind".
- Children, school uniforms, school logos, school gates, identifiable streets or cars.
- Any detail about the founder's family.

**Points to:** Fit Check (weekly hours, longest block, evenings/weekends questions), FIT Finder, Income Fit Test, max once a week.

### 4.4 Build Something Real

**Purpose:** Practical how-tos for testing an idea cheaply: the 14-day test, five real conversations, pricing a first offer, a one-sentence promise, reading Day 14 results.

**Audience job-to-be-done:** "Tell me exactly what to do next, in steps I can do this week."

**Formats:** Reels (step lists, notebook close-ups, typing on screen), carousels (templates and frameworks people save).

**Cadence in the calendar:** 6 Reels + 3 carousels over 30 days.

**Repeatable structure (beats):**

1. Hook: the mistake or the shortcut ("You don't need a website to test this.").
2. The steps (3–5), each one specific and doable.
3. A worked HYPOTHETICAL example where useful.
4. What counts as evidence and what doesn't.
5. Soft CTA (Fit Check's 14-day outline, or the Income Fit Test's dated plan, max once a week).

**Tone notes:** Clear, encouraging, no fluff. Like a friend who's done the boring bits.

**Done well:**

- Templates people can copy (a promise sentence, a conversation opener).
- "Stop" and "not enough evidence yet" treated as useful outcomes.
- Cheap first: no spending before evidence.

**Avoid:**

- Promising the test will lead to sales.
- Cold-messaging scripts as the only way to find customers (that's a red flag in our own Fit Check).
- Advising anything that needs a licence without "check the rules where you live".

**Points to:** Fit Check (14-day outline), FIT Finder, Income Fit Test, max once a week.

### 4.5 Inside the Empire

**Purpose:** Build trust by showing how Her Little Empire is built and run: what we're testing, what we don't know yet, how we label figures, why we're faceless but accountable.

**Audience job-to-be-done:** "Can I trust the people making this? Show me how you work."

**Formats:** Reels (desk, screens, hands, notebook, voice-over or text-on-screen), carousels (process explainers).

**Cadence in the calendar:** 6 Reels + 1 carousel over 30 days.

**Repeatable structure (beats):**

1. Hook: an honest admission or a process peek ("Here's what we don't know yet.").
2. What's true now (product exists, how cards are dated, how figures are labelled).
3. What's a plan or test, labelled as such.
4. What we'll record and where (no numbers published).
5. Soft CTA and sign-off.

**Tone notes:** Candid, plain, a little self-deprecating. No humble-brag.

**Done well:**

- Only things that are true, or clearly framed as PLAN / TEST.
- Screens showing non-identifying content: our own docs, the calculator, a blank calendar, a dated FIT Finder card.
- The seller identity line (ABN and email) where accountability is the point.

**Avoid:**

- Any results, sales, follower counts, reach or engagement figures.
- The founder's face, family, home exterior, location clues, or professional background.
- Showing email inboxes, customer names, order screens or analytics dashboards.

**Points to:** Fit Check, FIT Finder, and occasionally the Income Fit Test, max once a week.

## 5. Production rules

### 5.1 Faceless filming checklist

Allowed on camera: hands, desk, laptop or phone screen (non-identifying content only), notebook, printed Fit Check, pen, mug, kettle, calendar pages, window light, a bag on a chair, keys on a bench, a timer, cream/blush/aubergine props.

Before you film:

- [ ] No face, including reflections in the laptop, phone, window, kettle, glasses or mirror.
- [ ] No children, children's belongings with names, school uniforms, school logos, school bags with badges.
- [ ] No family photos, fridge notes, calendars with real names or appointments.
- [ ] No street view, house numbers, car number plates, landmarks, postcodes, letters with addresses.
- [ ] Rings, tattoos or distinctive nail art considered (fine if the founder is comfortable, but they can identify).
- [ ] Screens: browser tabs, bookmarks bar, notifications and account names hidden. Do Not Disturb on.
- [ ] Only our own materials on screen. No real person's post, profile or DM.
- [ ] No stock or scraped footage of people. No AI-generated people.

The founder may appear only as hands, desk, voice-over or text-on-screen. Never present an AI-generated person or voice as the founder or as a customer. If an AI voice is ever used, label it on screen and in the caption ("Voice-over: AI-generated voice").

### 5.2 Audio

- Default: original voice-over recorded by the founder, or no voice with on-screen text and quiet room sound.
- Music on unpaid posts: only from Instagram's licensed library, and only for posts that are not ads.
- Ads (including boosted posts): separately licensed audio only. Instagram's library audio is not assumed to be cleared for ads. Treat it as not cleared.
- No unlicensed music or footage, ever.

### 5.3 Privacy checks before posting

- [ ] Watch the full export frame by frame at 0.5x for reflections and screen details.
- [ ] Check the cover image and the first frame separately.
- [ ] Strip location tags. Never tag a place.
- [ ] Check captions and on-screen text for any family detail or location clue.
- [ ] Check the photo/video file has no location metadata (export fresh from the editor).

### 5.4 Accessibility

- **Captions on every Reel.** Burned-in captions for voice-over, checked by hand for spelling (Australian), plus Instagram's auto-captions switched on where available.
- On-screen text stays up long enough to read aloud twice.
- **Alt text on every carousel and Reel cover.** Formula:
  `[Format] on cream background. [What's shown, plainly]. Text reads: "[key line]". [Any number shown, with its label].`
  Example: `Carousel slide on cream background. Aubergine heading and a small blush underline. Text reads: "Feeling busy is not evidence." Below it, three numbered lines in a serif font.`
- **Contrast:** aubergine #43252c text on cream #f7f2e9 for all body text and headings. Blush #eacacb is for shapes, underlines and backgrounds behind aubergine text only, never for text on cream (too low contrast). Minimum on-screen text size: readable on a phone at arm's length; no more than about 25 words per slide.
- No information carried by colour alone (label "loss" in words, not just a colour).
- Avoid flashing or rapid strobe cuts.
- Emojis: few, and never in place of words. Screen readers read them aloud.

### 5.5 Brand look

- Background: cream #f7f2e9.
- Headings: aubergine #43252c, Bodoni-style serif.
- Accents: blush #eacacb.
- Numbers: Lora.
- Small footer on number-heavy slides: "HYPOTHETICAL example" or "FACT, checked [date]".

## 6. Caption formula

```
Line 1: The point, in one plain sentence (repeat or sharpen the hook).
Lines 2–5: The useful bit: the steps, the maths, or the question to ask.
Label line (if any number appears): HYPOTHETICAL example / FACT, checked [date].
One soft CTA (pick one rung from the CTA ladder).
Big plans. Real life.
```

Rules:

- Short paragraphs, one idea each.
- No hype words, no exclamation-mark stacks.
- Hashtags: see section 8.
- If the Income Fit Test price appears, include exactly: "Full refund within 14 days of purchase, no questions asked." (word for word, every time)

## 7. CTA ladder

Use one rung per post. Most posts use rungs 1–4. The Income Fit Test appears no more than once a week.

1. **Save:** "Save this for the next time someone sends you 'the opportunity'."
2. **Share:** "Send this to the friend who's been thinking about it."
3. **Comment with a real question:** "What's your longest reliable block of time this week? Tell me in the comments." (A genuine question we'll read. Not "comment YES".)
4. **Link in bio, free:** "The free 5-Question Fit Check is in the bio." / FIT Finder / calculator URL.
5. **Link in bio, paid (max once a week):** "The Income Fit Test is US$19. Full refund within 14 days of purchase, no questions asked."

"Comment a word to get a link by DM" is **not used**. It only works with a DM automation tool, which we don't have set up. Whether it affects reach is **UNKNOWN**. Revisit only if the founder sets one up.

No giveaways that require follows, tags or shares. No engagement bait ("tag three friends", "like if you agree").

## 8. Hashtag note

Keep it minimal: 0–3 relevant, plain hashtags per post (for example #sideincome #smallbusinessaustralia), or none. Whether hashtags change reach on Instagram is **UNKNOWN**. Test it and record what happens; don't assume. Never use hashtags tied to a scheme, a brand we don't own, or a trend we're not part of.

## 9. Pre-publish checklist (mirrors the hard rules)

Content claims

- [ ] No income claims or promised results. No figure of what someone will make.
- [ ] Every number labelled HYPOTHETICAL or FACT (checked date). Losses shown where relevant.
- [ ] No testimonials, reviews, customer quotes, follower counts, engagement stats or Meta/Instagram performance numbers.
- [ ] No quoted sentence followed by a dash and a capitalised name.
- [ ] No fake urgency, scarcity or deadlines.
- [ ] Any statement about how Instagram works is labelled UNKNOWN (or removed).
- [ ] If the US$19 price appears: exact refund sentence present, and it's the only paid mention this week.
- [ ] Only listed products referenced.
- [ ] No mention of the founder's professional background.
- [ ] Banned-words list checked (same patterns as the claims guard in `src/lib/ai/guard.ts`). Anti-scheme content uses "pay-to-join schemes", "schemes where you earn by signing people up", "the 'opportunity' DM", "starter kit you have to buy".

People and privacy

- [ ] Faceless: no face, reflections, children, family details, school logos or identifiable places.
- [ ] No AI-generated person or voice presented as the founder or a customer; any AI voice labelled.
- [ ] No real person, brand or post named, shown or recreated.
- [ ] No stock or scraped footage of people.

Rights

- [ ] Audio: original voice-over, or Instagram licensed library audio for non-ad posts only. Ads need separately licensed audio.
- [ ] All footage shot by us.

Access

- [ ] Captions burned in and checked.
- [ ] Alt text written using the formula.
- [ ] Contrast: aubergine text on cream, no blush text on cream.

Voice

- [ ] Reads like a text to a friend. Says the result, not the feature. Australian spelling.
- [ ] Ends with one soft CTA and, where natural, "Big plans. Real life."
