### a.3 Draft AI instruction for brand_blueprint (assembled only from the quotes above)

1. **Customer words first.** Collect exact phrases from reviews and forums for five similar products. Sort them into "the moment it hurts / the worry underneath / what she wishes would happen" and pick the three most frequent phrases (SIS).
2. **Ideal customer.** Ask 5 questions, then write one paragraph covering situation, struggle, what they've tried and what makes them say yes (AI pack 1.1).
3. **Positioning.** Fill the positioning card, including "Who it is NOT for" and "The catch or weakness I need to handle". Write the promise "I help [who, specifically] get [result] without [the thing they dread]" (SIS).
4. **Personality and tone.** State the voice in three words (example "calm, practical, kind") and one honest line about why it was made (SIS). Tone default from the About-me example: "warm, plain, friendly, no hype" (AI pack).
5. **Palette.** Three colours: one accent carries the brand and is used everywhere, with a cream/light neutral and a near-black (BiB + LOK). Dark text on light backgrounds (LOK).
6. **Typography.** Two fonts max: an elegant serif for headings and a clean sans for body, body text at least 11pt in print (BiB + LOK).
7. **Photo direction.** Show the real product, hands, desk or process, never the founder's face (SIS). Use daylight or natural light and a clean background (SIS, Get Found).
8. **Consistency rules.** "Use them the same way on every page" (LOK); "colours, fonts and tone match across my shop, posts and pins" (SIS).
9. **Honesty rules.** Real quotes only, with permission; real founding offers only; "usually" price only if real (BiB).

**Gaps the owner must supply:** how to choose the accent colour, the cream and near-black values, a voice/tone rubric beyond three words, and photo direction beyond lighting.

## c. Pricing: formulas, inputs, outputs, tests

### c.1 Price It Right: floor / market / value (Price_It_Right.pdf › Step 1)

**Inputs**
- `min_hourly_return`: "Take the hourly return you'd accept from the Reality Calculator" (an Income Fit Test tool, out of scope; in Empire Studio this would be a user input).
- `hours_total`: "Multiply by all the hours a job takes, including messages, admin and revisions."
- `costs`, `fees`: "Add costs and platform fees."
- `market_low`, `market_mid`, `market_high`: "Find five comparable offers your buyer could choose instead: same platform, same country, similar experience. Write down the lowest, middle and highest."
- `value` (qualitative): "What does the buyer save or gain? ... You don't charge all of it, but it tells you how much room you have."

**Formula (verbatim):** "Floor = (all hours × your minimum hourly return) + costs + fees"

**Decision rules (› How to choose)**
1. "If the floor is above the market's high, the option doesn't work at your hours yet. Change the offer (smaller scope, a product instead of a service), not your floor." Output: `change_offer`.
2. "If the floor sits between low and middle, start at the middle." Output: price = `market_mid`.
3. "Never price below the floor to 'get started'." Constraint: price ≥ floor.
4. "A founding price is fine if it is real: say exactly who gets it and when it ends ... then end it."
5. **Unknown:** what to do when the floor is between middle and high, or below the market low.

**Worked example (labelled "illustrative")**
"A bookkeeping clean-up takes 6 hours of work plus 2 hours of messages and set-up. Minimum return $35/hr × 8 hours → floor $280, plus any materials, software and platform fees on top. Five local offers range $250–$600, middle about $400. Price: $380 as a founding price for the first five clients, then $420."

Unit tests:
- `floor(hours=8, min_rate=35, costs=0, fees=0) == 280`
- with low = 250 and mid = 400, the floor (280) is in [low, mid], so the rule gives `start_at = 400`

Inconsistency: the example's founding $380 and then $420 depart from "start at the middle" (about $400). The guide gives no rule for founding discounts or for ending above the middle. **FLAG.**

**Price check after five customers (› After five customers).** Trigger: "Run this once you've had five paying customers, or at Day 14 if you've had at least two." Signal → meaning:
- "Almost everyone said yes without asking about price" → "You may be too cheap. A common rule of thumb is to raise by 15–25% for the next buyer (EST, not a rule)."
- "About half asked questions, most still bought" → "About right. Hold, and raise when your diary is full."
- "People liked the idea but went quiet at the price" → "Test a smaller package at a lower price before cutting your rate."
- "Your real hours were more than you quoted" → "Your floor was wrong. Re-run the Reality Calculator with real hours and re-price."
- "Repeat questions or extras on every job" → "Add them to 'Not included', or build them into a higher package."

Fields under "MY NUMBERS": "Price charged · Real hours per job · Real hourly return · Market middle · Next price ... from (date)". Feeds into: "A price that only works at hours you don't have is a 'change it' result, not a 'stop'."

**Payment terms (› Payment terms):**
- "Projects: 50% deposit before you start, balance on delivery, before final files are handed over."
- "Packages under $200: paid in full upfront."
- "Retainers: paid monthly in advance, with 30 days' notice to end."
- "Products: paid at checkout; let the platform handle refunds under its rules."

Price rise script: "(30 DAYS' NOTICE)".

### c.3 Side Income Money System: tax set-aside (› Your tax set-aside: how the calculator works)

"The formula, in plain words":
- "A. Income this month: From the Income log"
- "B. Expenses this month: From the Expense log (business amount)"
- "C. Profit: A minus B (shown as zero if below zero)"
- "D. My set-aside %: The figure you chose after checking"
- "E. Move to tax savings: C × D"

Detail (› How the tab calculates): A = "amount received plus fees, so it starts from gross sales". B = the "business amount column, including fees". Expense log: "Business amount = Total amount × business-use %" (› What to record).

Implementation:
```
A = Σ(amount_received + fees_deducted)
B = Σ(total_amount × business_use_pct)
C = max(A − B, 0)
E = C × D
```

**FLAG: possible fee double-handling.** Fees must be in B to avoid overstating profit. Whether they are copied automatically ("the sheet can carry them across") or entered twice is unknown, because the spreadsheet isn't in the folder.

Unit tests (derived, not in source): A = 1000, B = 300, D = 0.25 → E = 175. A = 200, B = 300 → E = 0.

Rule: "We don't recommend a set-aside percentage." D must be user input.

Threshold watch: "Share of threshold used (it turns pink at 75%)" = turnover so far ÷ threshold. Country thresholds (FACT, checked 17/19 Oct 2026): AU GST A$75,000; UK VAT £90,000; Canada GST/HST $30,000 over four consecutive quarters; NZ GST $60,000; US: UNKNOWN (per state), entered in "My own threshold" (› Registration and threshold watch). "Turnover means total sales before costs, not profit."

### c.4 Real hourly return (three products agree, one presentation detail differs)

- 90-day-grow-plan › The three numbers: "(Money received - all costs) divided by all hours spent". Example: "you receive $300, costs are $45, and you spent 12 hours in total. ($300 - $45) / 12 = $21.25 an hour. If you only counted delivery time (6 hours), you would think it was double."
  - Unit tests: `rhr(300, 45, 12) == 21.25`; `rhr(300, 45, 6) == 42.50`.
- 14_Day_Tracker.pdf › Day 14 summary: "Real hourly return so far (received − spent) ÷ hours". The Tracker records minutes ("Total minutes spent (add the daily boxes)"), so hours = minutes ÷ 60 (implied, not stated).
- ai-business-assistant-pack › 7.2: "I charge [price]. Costs per sale are [costs]. Total time per sale ... is [hours]. Work out my real hourly return ... Then show what changes if I reduce time by 20% or raise the price by [amount]. Label these as examples."
